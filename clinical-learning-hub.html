⚖️ Medico-legal & Ethics Quick Guide

  • Consent: explain purpose, material risks, alternatives and the right to decline; assess capacity, document consent, and follow rules for minors/emergencies.
  • Records: write promptly, factually, legibly and with date/time and author; distinguish patient report from observed findings; correct transparently—never obscure the original entry.
  • Confidentiality: share minimum necessary information only for lawful care; protect records and avoid identifiers in study notes.
  • Death certification: follow local law and institutional policy; certify only when authorised and within competence; refer uncertain, sudden, violent or otherwise reportable deaths through the required pathway.
  • Statutory duties: safeguarding, notifiable conditions, medico-legal cases and mandatory reporting vary by jurisdiction; consult current local law/senior staff.

Educational summary only; requirements differ by jurisdiction and change over time.

💊 Emergency & Red-Flag Triage

Call emergency services / urgent senior review for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms, airway/breathing/circulation compromise, altered consciousness, shock, uncontrolled bleeding or suspected time-critical illness.

  • Chest pain: ongoing severe pressure/pain, breathlessness, collapse, sweating, radiation, or new symptoms with instability.
  • Acute abdomen: sudden severe pain, rigid abdomen, fainting/shock, persistent vomiting, blood, pregnancy possibility with pain/bleeding.
  • Child: difficulty breathing, blue/grey colour, hard to wake, seizure, dehydration, non-blanching rash, or caregiver concern with deterioration.
  • Obstetrics: heavy bleeding, seizure, severe headache/visual symptoms, severe abdominal pain, breathlessness, collapse, or reduced fetal movement per local pathway.

Do not use this abbreviated list to rule out emergencies. Homoeopathic study never replaces urgent assessment or evidence-based treatment.

Clinical Examination

Structured checklists for supervised practice. Normal values are approximate adult resting references; age, pregnancy, altitude, method and local lab ranges matter.

General Examination — head-to-toe

Common auscultation landmarks

  • Aortic: 2nd right intercostal space, right sternal edge.
  • Pulmonary: 2nd left intercostal space, left sternal edge.
  • Erb area: 3rd left intercostal space, sternal edge.
  • Tricuspid: lower left sternal edge (4th–5th intercostal space).
  • Mitral: apex, usually 5th left intercostal space near mid-clavicular line.
  • Lungs: compare matching zones front, sides and back; include bases.

Systemic Examination Checklist

RespiratoryInspection → palpation (trachea, expansion, tactile vocal fremitus) → percussion → auscultation (breath sounds, added sounds, vocal resonance); compare corresponding zones.
CardiovascularPulse and BP → JVP → precordial inspection/palpation (apex, heaves, thrills) → auscultation at valve areas, with position/maneuvers only when appropriate → peripheral pulses/edema.
AbdominalPosition, exposure and consent → inspect → auscultate → percuss → palpate → organ-specific maneuvers when indicated; consider hernial or rectal examination only with consent, indication and supervision.
NeurologicHigher functions → cranial nerves → motor (bulk, tone, power) → reflexes → sensory → coordination and gait; focus to presentation.
MusculoskeletalLook, feel, move, function; compare sides and assess neurovascular status. Use a focused joint/spine exam.

OBGYN Case-Taking Add-on

Menstrual history (LMP, cycle, duration, flow, pain); obstetric history (gravidity/parity, outcomes, complications); pregnancy possibility and contraception; gynecologic symptoms, discharge/bleeding, sexual history only when relevant and sensitively obtained; screening, past history, medications/allergies and relevant family/social history. Explain intimate examinations, obtain specific consent, offer chaperone and follow local safeguarding pathways.

Use age-appropriate, trauma-informed, confidential practice and local protocols.
